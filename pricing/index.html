QA Outsourcing Pricing: How a Remote QA Team Works and What It Costs

One model — a remote QA team, embedded in your sprints or running testing end to end — billed hourly at $18–45/hr depending on experience and skill. No fixed-price quotes, no project packages. Here's exactly what moves the number.

One model: a remote QA team

QAInfinity doesn't sell fixed-price projects or software licenses — we staff a remote QA team and bill for the hours it works. Two ways to use it:

  • Embedded engineers. QA engineers and SDETs join your existing team, attend your standups, work your sprint cadence, and report to your leads.
  • End-to-end testing. QAInfinity plans, executes, and reports on testing for a feature, release, or product area, with a single lead as your point of contact.

Either way, you're billed for the hours the team actually works — not a flat project fee, and not a software seat license.

Two scheduling options, any time zone

The rate is the same either way; which one you pick is about workflow, not cost.

  • Your own hours. The team works your team's hours for full daily overlap, wherever you are.
  • The 24-hour cycle. Your day ends, QA tests overnight, and bugs and status are waiting when your day starts — with a 1–2 hour overlap window for sync.

What the rate actually depends on

$18–45/hr by experience and skill — a range, not a quote

The number moves on two things:

  • Seniority. Junior and mid-level QA engineers run toward $18–30/hr; senior QA engineers and SDETs toward $30–45/hr.
  • Skill mix. Manual/functional testing sits at the lower end of its band; automation engineering, test architecture, and specialized domains (API, performance, AI/LLM evals) push toward the top of the senior band.

There's no separate "AI tooling" surcharge: when Loopsy (our in-house automation platform) is part of how a team works, that's reflected in the engineer's skill tier, not billed as an add-on line item.

Typical team shapes

  • A single embedded engineer — the most common starting point, slotted into one squad or product area.
  • A small embedded team (2–4 engineers) — usually a mix of manual and automation skill, covering a full product surface across releases.
  • An end-to-end team for a release or feature — sized to the scope at hand, with QAInfinity's lead doing the day-to-day planning and reporting.

Team size and mix typically get set in the first 30 days once the real backlog and release cadence are visible, and can flex up or down as the engagement runs.

What's included, and what isn't

  • Included: mutual NDA and IP assignment before any code or test asset is shared; access and tooling provisioned to your existing stack; a start inside 5–10 business days of a signed SOW.
  • Not included: a fixed-price or project-based quote, a software license fee, or a retainer billed regardless of hours worked. You pay for the hours your team actually uses.

Quick answers

Is $18–45/hr a quote or a range?
It's a range. The actual rate for your engagement depends on the seniority and skill mix your project needs, and is confirmed before any work starts — never after.
Do the two scheduling options cost different amounts?
The published range doesn't change based on which scheduling option you choose. Which one fits your team is a workflow decision, not a pricing one.
Is there a fixed-price or project-based option?
No. Every engagement is billed hourly against the $18–45/hr range above, whether it's one embedded engineer or a full end-to-end team.
