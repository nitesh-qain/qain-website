Offshore QA Testing in Your Time Zone

One remote QA team, two ways to run the schedule: your own business hours for full daily overlap, or the 24-hour cycle — your developers hand off at day's end, QA tests through the night, and bugs and status are waiting before you're back. Here's what that actually looks like from your city, not a generic "we work around the clock" claim.

What your city's offset actually means

India Standard Time (IST) is fixed at UTC+5:30 year-round — it never observes daylight saving. Every market below does, which is exactly where a vague "we cover your hours" pitch tends to fall apart once the clocks change twice a year. These are the real offsets, and which scheduling option fits each one best.

Ranges cover both sides of daylight saving. US/Canada and UK/Europe move their clocks forward Mar–Nov (narrowing the US gap and the UK/Europe gap by an hour during that window) and back Nov–Mar; Australia and New Zealand are on the opposite cycle (daylight saving roughly Oct–Apr, widening their IST gap during that stretch). Singapore has no daylight saving, so its offset never moves. UK/Australia figures match the arithmetic already published on our Europe page and in A Buyer's Guide to QA Outsourcing Time Zones.

Two ways to use the gap, same rate

Which option fits depends on how far your city sits from IST, not on cost — the rate is the same either way.

  • Your own hours. For London, Berlin, Singapore, and (on a shifted block) Sydney, Melbourne, and Auckland, the offset is small enough that a scheduled India working block lands live, same-day overlap with your team — natural for the smaller offsets, a deliberately shifted block for the larger ones.
  • The 24-hour cycle. For New York, San Francisco, Toronto, and Vancouver, the gap is large enough that same-day overlap would mean working the middle of an India night. Instead, your developers hand off at day's end, QA tests through the India working day while your team is offline, and bugs and status are filed before you're back — with a short 1–2 hour overlap window built in for sync. The mechanics are the same pattern already covered in The Follow-the-Sun QA Handoff.

Either way: a remote QA team at $18–45/hr by experience and skill, NDA and IP assignment as standard, and engagements starting within 5–10 business days. See the full breakdown on Pricing or Dedicated QA Engineers.

Quick answers

Does an India-based QA team really work across every time zone above?
Yes — the working block (natural-overlap or shifted) is scheduled per engagement at kickoff, matched to your actual city and season, not a single fixed default applied to every client regardless of where they are.
Why is the 24-hour cycle only recommended for US/Canada cities here?
Below roughly an 8-hour gap, a shifted India working block can still land live, same-day overlap with your team. Past that — the 9.5–13.5 hour gap to US and Canada — same-day overlap would mean working the middle of an India night, so the 24-hour handoff is the better fit: your day ends, ours begins.
Does daylight saving actually change which option I should use?
It shifts the exact overlap window by an hour twice a year, but not which option fits — a city on the "your hours" side of the table stays there whether it's standard or daylight time; we adjust the scheduled block each time the clocks change rather than leaving it fixed.
