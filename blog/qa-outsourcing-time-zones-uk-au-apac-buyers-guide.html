A Buyer's Guide to QA Outsourcing Time Zones: Working Across UK/AU/APAC Hours

Every QA-outsourcing pitch promises "round-the-clock coverage." The question that actually determines whether an engagement works is narrower and far more checkable: how many real-time overlap hours will you have with an India-based team, and what's the plan for everything outside them? Here's the real time-zone math for UK, Australian, and APAC buyers — and the two scheduling models that make it work in practice.

2–4 hrsthe live overlap window time-zone-management research generally agrees is enough for a daily standup and real-time defect triage — everything else runs async

Why "24/7 coverage" is the wrong thing to ask for

Every outsourcing vendor will tell you their team works around the clock. That's true and also not the point — a tester who's awake at 3am your time is only useful if there's a live window for a question to get asked and answered the same day. Time-zone-management guidance converges on a smaller, more useful number: a 2–4 hour live overlap, protected for standups and real-time problem-solving, with everything outside it handled through written updates, recorded handoffs, and clear response-time expectations rather than left informal.

The reverse framing is where the value actually sits: a time-zone gap, handled deliberately, becomes a follow-the-sun handoff rather than a dead zone — QA runs while your team sleeps, and you come back online to triaged, already-tested work instead of a queue. That only works, though, once you know your actual offset.

See QASource's guide to managing offshore QA teams and Brainhub's analysis of coping with time-zone differences in offshoring for the overlap-window guidance referenced above.

The actual math: India Standard Time against UK, Australian, and APAC hours

India Standard Time (IST) doesn't observe daylight saving, so it's a fixed point — UTC+5:30, every day of the year. The buyer-side time zones move with the seasons, which is exactly where "we offer time-zone overlap" marketing copy tends to get vague. Here's the actual arithmetic:

Australia runs AEDT (UTC+11) during daylight saving, roughly October–April, and AEST (UTC+10) the rest of the year — see Second Talent's Asia–Europe overlap-hours guide for how these offsets are typically scheduled against for remote teams. UK/Europe figures match the offset already published on our UK & Europe region page.

Two scheduling models, not one default

The table above splits into two genuinely different scheduling problems, and a vendor that treats them identically is the first warning sign:

  • Natural-overlap markets (UK, Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, and most of APAC). The offset is small enough that a standard India working day already lines up with a standard local working day, end to end. Nothing needs to be negotiated around a large gap — this is the model we run for UK & Europe engagements.
  • Shifted-block markets (Australia, and the same pattern used for US engagements). The offset is large enough that a default 9-to-6 IST day mostly misses the local business day. The fix is a deliberately earlier (or later) IST working block, agreed at kickoff as part of the SOW — not a fixed default that happens to suit one market and gets improvised for the next.
Most outsourcing failures are communication failures before they're competence failures — and most communication failures trace back to an overlap window nobody actually agreed to in writing.

What to ask before you sign

Three questions cut through most of the vagueness in a QA-outsourcing pitch:

  • What's the exact scheduled overlap window, in your time zone, in writing? "We cover your hours" isn't an answer; a start and end time is.
  • What happens to a defect found outside that window? A good answer names a specific async path — written handoff, recorded walkthrough, a defined response-time SLA — not "we'll get to it."
  • Is the overlap a fixed default, or set per engagement? A vendor running the same block for a UK client and an Australian client either hasn't noticed the offset is different, or hasn't bothered to adjust for it.

QAInfinity schedules the working block — natural-overlap or shifted — as part of kickoff for every engagement, matched to the actual market, not a fixed default that happens to work for one coast or one country.

Quick answers

How many overlap hours do I actually need with an outsourced QA team?
Time-zone-management research generally converges on 2–4 live overlap hours as enough for a daily standup and real-time defect triage, with everything outside that window handled async through written updates and recorded handoffs. More overlap isn't the goal in itself — a clear, protected window beats a wide but undefined one.
Does an India-based QA team work during UK business hours?
Yes, without any shifted schedule. India Standard Time runs 4.5–5.5 hours ahead of UK time depending on the season (GMT in winter, BST in summer), small enough that a standard India working day already covers a full UK working day end to end.
How does time-zone overlap work for Australian buyers specifically?
Australia's offset from India is larger and seasonal — 4.5 hours (AEST) or 5.5 hours (AEDT, during Australian daylight saving from October to April) ahead of IST. A standard 9-to-6 India schedule mostly misses the Australian morning, so the fix is the same shifted-block model used for US engagements: deliberately move the India team's working hours earlier, agreed at kickoff.
