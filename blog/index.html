QA Outsourcing Isn't One Thing: Managed Team vs. Staff Augmentation vs. Contract-to-Hire

Three different arrangements get pitched as one — here's who owns the risk, who runs the day-to-day work, and which model actually fits where your team is right now.

QA OutsourcingEngagement ModelsBuyer's Guide
Staff augmentation
Engineers join your team under your own leadership — you keep control and build in-house QA knowledge, and the risk of using them well sits with you.

A Buyer's Guide to QA Outsourcing Time Zones: Working Across UK/AU/APAC Hours

The real time-zone math between India and UK, Australian, and APAC business hours — and the two scheduling models that make live overlap actually happen.

QA OutsourcingTime ZonesUK & AU & APAC
The real question
Not "24/7 coverage," but a specific, written overlap window — 2–4 live hours is generally enough for standups and real-time triage, with the rest handled async.
The math
IST is a fixed UTC+5:30. UK runs 4.5–5.5 hrs behind (natural overlap, no shift needed); Singapore/Malaysia/Philippines run 2.5 hrs ahead; Australia runs 4.5–5.5 hrs ahead, seasonally — needing a deliberately shifted IST block, same model as US engagements.
The takeaway
Ask for the exact scheduled window in writing, what happens to defects found outside it, and whether that window is a fixed default or set per engagement.

The Follow-the-Sun QA Handoff: A 24-Hour Dev Cycle, Not an Overnight Gap

How a short daily sync turns an offshore time-zone gap into round-the-clock progress — devs ship, QA tests overnight, devs return to tested code.

QA OutsourcingProcessUS & UK Teams
The pattern
QA is scheduled in a time zone roughly opposite the dev team's, with a short 1–2 hour daily overlap for handoff and sync — then QA tests what shipped that day while developers are offline.
Why it works
The value isn't the overlap hours themselves; it's the many hours QA is working while the dev team is asleep, with bugs and status already filed by the time developers are back online.
Where it fits
Strongest for US teams, where the India time-zone offset is large; UK/Europe teams already get near-full-day overlap directly, so the handoff matters most at the edges of the day.
