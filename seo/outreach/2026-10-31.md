## Helpful answer draft (Q&A style, affiliation disclosed)

**Platform:** Stack Exchange — Software Quality Assurance & Testing (or a close Reddit equivalent, e.g. r/QualityAssurance)

**Question it answers (realistic, commonly asked):** "How do you actually run QA when your QA team is 8+ time zones away from your dev team? Does anything other than 'just deal with the lag' work?"

**Draft answer:**

> Disclosure: I work at QAInfinity, a QA outsourcing company, so take the "we do this" parts with that in mind — but the mechanics below apply whether you outsource QA or just have a distributed in-house team.
>
> The pattern that actually works is usually called "follow-the-sun" and it's a scheduling decision, not a tooling one:
>
> 1. **Keep a short daily sync in the overlap window**, even if it's only 1–2 hours — this is for triage and handoff, not status theater. Agree what's "done enough to test" before anyone logs off.
> 2. **QA tests against what was actually committed that day**, not a stale branch — so devs come back to a tested, current backlog instead of a backlog that's a day old by the time they see results.
> 3. **Write the handoff down, not just said once in standup** — a short end-of-day note (what shipped, what's risky, what to watch) travels better across a time-zone gap than a verbal handoff that only one side remembers.
> 4. **Separate "time-zone lag" from "distance lag"** — a lot of what people blame on time zones is actually just unclear handoff documentation; fixing the documentation habit removes most of the pain even before you touch scheduling.
>
> Where this breaks down: if your dev and QA teams have close to zero overlap *and* no handoff discipline, you'll feel like you're always one day behind. Where it doesn't break down: a 3.5–5.5 hour offset (which is what UK/EU-to-India or Japan/Korea-to-India usually looks like) gives you a full working-day overlap window if you actually use it, which is closer to synchronous than most people assume going in.
>
> Happy to go deeper on any piece of this if useful.
