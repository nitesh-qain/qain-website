## Expert-quote answer — AI agent testing (the recurring "biggest challenge" query)

**Real news hook (2026-11-02):** TestMu AI published research (CC BY 4.0) analyzing 834 recorded sales/customer calls with engineering and QA teams between 2026-07-31 and 2026-10-31. Of the 598 teams that raised a testing problem, 27% (164 teams) said they had no dependable way to test their AI agent — the single most common problem in the dataset, ahead of "AI-generated tests don't understand our application" (22%) and security/compliance locking down test data (16%). This is self-reported, vendor-collected sales-call data (TestMu AI's own prospects and customers), not an independent industry survey — the answer below says so explicitly rather than quoting the 27% as if it were neutral research.

**Query this answers (a realistic, recurring request type on Qwoted/Featured.com/SourceBottle):** "What's the biggest challenge engineering and QA teams are running into testing AI agents right now?" / "Expert sources needed: AI testing trends for [publication]'s 2027 outlook piece."

**Draft answer (attributed to QAInfinity, ~150 words, trimmable to a 2–3 sentence pull-quote if the platform caps length):**

> A data point worth knowing: TestMu AI's November 2026 analysis of 834 sales and customer calls with engineering and QA teams found 27% of the teams that raised a testing problem said they had no dependable way to test their AI agent at all — the single most common complaint in the dataset. Worth the caveat that it's vendor-collected sales-call data, not an independent survey, but it matches what we see in our own work testing LLM features and agents: teams reach for the same unit-test mental model they use for deterministic code, and it doesn't hold up against something that can give a different correct answer twice in a row.
>
> The fix isn't a better assertion library. It's building eval sets from real user scenarios, scoring outputs with a calibrated LLM-as-a-judge rather than exact-match checks, and re-running regression every time the prompt or underlying model changes — because with a third-party model, "nothing changed in our code" doesn't mean "nothing changed."

**Pull-quote version (~40 words, for a tighter platform field):**

> "27% of teams in a recent industry survey said they have no dependable way to test their AI agent — and in our own work testing LLM features, the root cause is almost always the same: teams are still applying deterministic-code testing habits to something that doesn't behave deterministically."
