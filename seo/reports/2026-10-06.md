## Research findings
This was an execution day, not a dedicated Phase 3 research day, but the blog post required grounding research:
- **Staff augmentation vs. managed services (control & risk):** the real distinction is who runs day-to-day work and who owns outcome risk, not price tier. Staff aug = you keep control, you own the risk of using the talent well, knowledge builds in-house. Managed team = vendor runs it against SLAs, vendor owns outcome risk, less process knowledge stays with you. — [Cortance's 2026 decision framework](https://cortance.com/blog/comparison-vs/staff-augmentation-vs-managed-services-the-2026-decision-framework), [QualityLogic's QA-specific version](https://qualitylogic.com/knowledge-center/qa-staff-augmentation-vs-qa-managed-services), [Kanerika's comparison](https://kanerika.com/blogs/staff-augmentation-vs-managed-services/)
- **Market split (Gartner's 2026 IT Services Framework, via Cortance/Kanerika):** staff augmentation ≈ 61% of engagement volume, managed services ≈ 22%, but managed services carries 3–5x the average contract value; 58% of enterprise IT engagements in 2026 run a hybrid of the two rather than picking one.
- **Contract-to-Hire structure:** a bounded 30–90 day trial, candidate on the vendor's payroll first, risk of an "unknown hire" sits with the vendor during the trial — distinct from staff aug/managed-team as an ongoing-engagement shape; it's a hiring-risk tool, not a third shape. — [Arc.dev](https://arc.dev/employer-blog/contract-to-hire-flexible-remote-engineering-team-scaling/), [ActivatedScale](https://www.activatedscale.com/feeds/blog/contract-to-hire-vs-permanent)
- **Stage-fit:** pre-PMF/evolving-scope teams without in-house QA leadership fit staff aug best (fast ramp, redirects with the team); scaling teams past founder-led QA fit managed team (less day-to-day overhead, SLA-defined scope); a specific pending hire fits Contract-to-Hire regardless of stage. — [DeviQA's QA-for-scale-ups guidance](https://www.deviqa.com/services/qa-services-for-scale-ups/), [Second Talent's comparison](https://www.secondtalent.com/it-staffing/staff-augmentation-vs-managed-services/)

## LinkedIn draft (ready to post — not posted)

> Most "QA outsourcing" pitches collapse three genuinely different arrangements into one sales conversation.
>
> Staff augmentation, managed teams, and Contract-to-Hire aren't the same thing at different price points — they answer three different questions:
>
> → Staff augmentation: engineers work inside *your* team, under *your* process. You keep control, you build in-house QA knowledge, and you own the risk of using the talent well.
>
> → A managed team: a vendor runs a defined QA scope against SLAs. Less day-to-day management for you — but less of that knowledge stays in-house.
>
> → Contract-to-Hire: not really a third "ongoing engagement" shape at all. It's a bounded 30–90 day trial for one specific question — should this person join your payroll — with the vendor carrying the hiring risk until you've seen real work.
>
> Per Gartner's 2026 IT Services Framework, 58% of enterprise engagements now run a hybrid of these models. None of them is a universal default — the right one depends on what's actually uncertain about your QA function right now.
