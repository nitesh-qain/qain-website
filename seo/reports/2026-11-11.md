## Research findings
Quick web search to ground the page's checklist in current (2026) e-commerce QA practice:
- **Checkout and payments**: test against the payment processor's own sandbox and test cards, never real card data in a test environment; cover async outcomes (delayed confirmations, redirects) and the "unhappy path" (refunds, cancellations, disputes), not just the happy path. ([Mindbowser](https://www.mindbowser.com/qa-payment-gateway-testing/), [BrowserStack](https://www.browserstack.com/guide/ecommerce-app-testing-techniques-and-approaches))
- **Mobile**: a checkout that works on desktop Chrome can still fail on mobile Safari — a gap that's easy to miss if mobile isn't treated as its own test pass. ([ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/ecommerce-application-testing-the-ultimate-guide/))
- **Regression cadence**: run the revenue-critical path plus whatever a change touched on every release/merge, rather than relying on an occasional full manual sweep. ([ybug](https://ybug.io/blog/ecommerce-website-qa-checklist), [DeviQA](https://www.deviqa.com/blog/a-brief-guide-to-ecommerce-testing/))
- **Data accuracy**: stock, pricing and promotions change constantly, so a page loading isn't proof the data on it is correct at that moment. ([TestDevLab](https://www.testdevlab.com/blog/e-commerce-testing-musts))
- **Third-party dependencies**: payment gateways and shipping providers are common failure points outside the client's own code, and are usually called out as needing direct integration testing, with CI/CD integration recommended. ([A1QA](https://www.a1qa.com/industries/ecommerce), [DCKAP](https://www.dckap.com/ecommerce-qa-services.htm))
- Competitor scan: DeviQA, A1QA, DCKAP, Testlio, Testriq, TestFort and QATestLab all run dedicated e-commerce QA pages built around checkout/payment testing as the headline, with cart behavior, cross-browser/device coverage and compliance-framework references (PCI DSS, 3DS 2.0) as supporting areas. Per the Phase 6 no-compliance-wording guardrail, our page covers the same testing ground (sandbox payment testing, etc.) without naming any compliance framework or certification we don't hold.
- Caveat: sources are predominantly vendor marketing; no statistics were pulled from them into the page, only the general shape of what to test — all figures on the page itself trace to the published Angara case study.
