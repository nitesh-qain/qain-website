## Research findings
The companion blog post required its own research pass (see citations above). A few notes worth keeping for future LLM-evaluation content:
- The foundational LLM-as-a-judge literature (Zheng et al., G-Eval) is from 2023 and still the most-cited reference point — newer work mostly refines or stress-tests it (bias studies, contextual/grounded benchmarks) rather than overturning the core finding that a strong judge model can approach human-level agreement on open-ended tasks.
- The gap between "judges agree with humans ~80% of the time" (open-ended chat) and "~55% consistent accuracy" (context-grounded grading, per ContextualJudgeBench) is the single most useful nuance for a technical reader — most vendor content quotes the former without the latter's caveat.
- Real bias auditing (position/length/self-preference testing) appears to be genuinely rare in production deployments per the 2026 healthcare scoping review, which is a useful honest note rather than implying every team already does this rigorously.

> → Calibration isn't optional. Raw percent agreement with human reviewers can look great and still be meaningless on imbalanced data — chance-corrected measures like Cohen's kappa are the real test.
