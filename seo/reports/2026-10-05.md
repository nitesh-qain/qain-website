## Research findings

Grounded today's post in fresh 2026 sources (all cited inline in the post):
- **CI/CD integration:** a commit triggers the agent, which analyzes the diff, selects relevant test scenarios, executes across web/API/integration layers, and updates coverage — a different shape than scheduled full-suite automation (Ampcome, Shiplight).
- **Compression numbers:** full test cycles reportedly dropping from days to ~2 hours, with ~40% coverage growth within a month of GenAI-embedded QA (ThinkSys 2026 QA Trends Report).
- **Multi-agent architecture:** 2026 research has moved from single-agent to coordinated multi-agent testing systems (planning/generation/execution/triage specialists); one microservices architecture study reported a 38% reduction in manual QA effort (two 2026 arXiv papers).
- **Maintenance tax:** Google's own large-scale study found ~16% of tests show flakiness at some point; self-healing test automation (now a $24B+ market) is reported to cut that maintenance time by 75–95% (ContextQA).
- **Market/pricing shift:** the outsourced testing market is ~$50B in 2026 and pricing is splitting into traditional hourly ($25–$90/hr), outcome/coverage-based, and flat-fee AI-native QaaS models — a real framing shift for how buyers should evaluate a QA partner (Virtustant and others).

Full source links are inline in the post itself (`blog/agentic-qa-2026-release-cycle.html`).
