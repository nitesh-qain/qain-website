> Most payment bugs don't look like bugs. They look like a number that's quietly wrong.
>
> We published our approach to testing fintech and payment products this week: `/industries/fintech/` plus a companion post on ledgers, retries and reconciliation.
>
> The pattern that stood out doing the research: an idempotency key stops a retry from becoming a second charge — that part's well understood, and Stripe's own docs spell it out clearly. What's less talked about is the quieter failure underneath it: a duplicate webhook that doesn't double-charge the customer, but still inserts a second ledger row for the same event. Nothing fails visibly. The number is just wrong, and it usually isn't caught until someone reconciles the books weeks later.
>
> That's the case for testing reconciliation on a schedule, not as a one-off check after a release looks fine — and for testing sandbox coverage honestly, since card-issuer challenge flows behave differently with a real card in the loop than they do in a sandbox.
